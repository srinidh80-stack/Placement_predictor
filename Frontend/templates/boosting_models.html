Boosting Fundamentals

AdaBoost adaptive re-weighting vs. Gradient Boosting gradient-space descent.

1. Decision Stumps 2. Pseudo-Residuals 3. Staged Accuracy

💡 Fundamentals Highlights

  • AdaBoost: Sequentially increases weights of misclassified records; aggregates with $\alpha_m$ vote.
  • Gradient Boosting: Fits each subsequent tree to the negative gradient (residuals) of the loss function.
  • Progression: Accuracy improves stage by stage as weak learners specialize on hard cases.

Learning Rate & Shrinkage

Balancing convergence speed and generalization with shrinkage factor $\eta$.

1. Shrinkage ($\eta$) 2. Overfitting Control 3. Convergence Rate

💡 Learning Rate Insights

  • Too High ($\eta \ge 0.5$): Overshoots minimum; early plateau or overfitting.
  • Moderate ($\eta = 0.1$): Stable convergence across 100 stages.
  • Small ($\eta \le 0.05$): Requires more estimators but yields lower variance and better test generalization.

Modern Boosted Trees

State-of-the-art benchmarks: XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost speed vs accuracy.

1. XGBoost 2. LightGBM 3. CatBoost

💡 Modern Framework Differences

  • XGBoost: 2nd order Taylor expansion (gradients + Hessians) with depth-wise growth.
  • LightGBM: Best-first (leaf-wise) split strategy with histogram binning for ultra fast training.
  • CatBoost: Symmetric oblivious trees and target statistics handling with minimal hyperparameter tuning.

FOLDER 02 • HYPERPARAMETER TUNING

Learning Rate (Shrinkage) & Convergence Analysis

Shrinkage Factor $\eta$

Shrinkage scales each tree's contribution by $0 < \eta \le 1$: $F_m(x) = F_{m-1}(x) + \eta \cdot \gamma_m h_m(x)$. A lower learning rate forces the model to take smaller, more cautious steps, preventing individual trees from over-committing to noise and producing lower test error.
